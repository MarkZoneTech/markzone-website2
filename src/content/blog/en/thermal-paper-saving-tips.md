Receipt paper is one of those costs nobody thinks about until the roll runs out in the middle of a busy evening. Individually, rolls are cheap. Across a year, in several tills, with wasted prints and faded receipts, they are not. A few simple habits save paper, money and frustration.

## Buy the right roll for your printer

Thermal printers take specific widths, usually 80 mm or 58 mm, and a maximum roll diameter. Buying the wrong size wastes money, and forcing a roll that does not fit causes jams. Check what your printer takes and keep a note of it near the counter so whoever orders supplies gets it right.

Quality matters too. Very cheap paper can leave dust inside the printer, print faintly, or fade quickly. A slightly better roll that prints clearly and lasts is usually cheaper overall.

## Store rolls properly

Thermal paper reacts to heat. In the UAE climate, that matters:

- **Keep rolls away from direct sunlight** and hot storerooms.
- **Do not store them in a car** or next to equipment that gets warm.
- **Keep them away from chemicals**, such as cleaning products, that can discolour the paper.
- **Use older stock first**, so rolls do not sit for months.

The same goes for receipts your customers or your accountant need to keep. Heat and sunlight fade them.

## Stop unnecessary prints

Wasted receipts come from a few common habits:

- **Reprinting "just in case"** when the first print was fine
- **Printing test pages** repeatedly while troubleshooting
- **Printing a receipt nobody wants**

A simple question at the counter, such as "Would you like a receipt?", avoids many unwanted prints. For customers who need a copy later, it helps if the sale is saved properly in your system so it can be found and reprinted when genuinely needed.

## Keep receipts short and clear

A receipt should show what the customer needs: shop details, items, prices, VAT and payment. Long blocks of text, oversized logos and empty space use more paper on every sale. ## Look after the printer

A dirty print head produces faint receipts, which often leads to reprints. Follow the manufacturer’s cleaning guidance, keep the paper compartment free of dust, and replace worn parts when the manufacturer recommends it. A well-maintained printer wastes less paper.

## Track the cost

Add receipt rolls to your expenses like any other running cost. When expenses sit in the same system as sales, you can see whether paper use is growing faster than your sales, which is often a sign of reprints or waste.

## Small savings, every day

None of these steps is dramatic on its own. Together, they mean fewer rolls, fewer jams and clearer receipts.
