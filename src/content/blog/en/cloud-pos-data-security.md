Many shop owners still keep their business records on a single computer at the counter, or in a notebook in the drawer. It feels safe because it is physically close. But a single computer can fail, be damaged or go missing, and a notebook can be lost or misread. Moving records online is a big step, and it is fair to ask whether it is safe.

The right answer is not "the cloud is always safe" or "the cloud is always risky." It depends on the provider and on your own habits. Here is what to ask, and what to do on your side.

## What is really at risk?

Your business data includes sales, stock, purchases, supplier details, staff salaries and customer information such as names and phone numbers. Losing it disrupts your business. Exposing it can damage customer trust. Both matter.

## Questions to ask any software provider

1. **Where does the system run, and who builds it?** Understand whether the provider develops and runs its own platform or relies on others.
2. **Who at the provider can access my data, and why?** Access should be limited to what is needed to support you.
3. **How do I control my staff’s access?** You should decide who sees what.
4. **How is my data backed up, and how would it be restored?** Ask for a plain explanation, not jargon.
5. **What happens if I stop using the service?** You should be able to get your records, for example as reports exported to PDF or Excel.

## Habits that protect your data

Much of data safety sits with the business itself:

- **Give every staff member their own login.** Shared logins make it impossible to know who did what.
- **Use roles and permissions.** A cashier does not need access to salaries or supplier prices. [OxPOS](/oxpos) supports staff roles and permissions, and our other apps include staff permissions too.
- **Remove access immediately** when someone leaves.
- **Use strong passwords** and do not write them on the counter.
- **Keep devices updated** and locked when not in use.
- **Export important reports regularly**, such as monthly sales and VAT figures, and keep them safely.

## Customer information deserves care

Customer names, phone numbers and purchase histories are valuable, and customers trust you with them. Only collect what you need, keep access limited, and never share customer lists casually, including over WhatsApp groups.

## The advantage of being organized

A well-organized system is often safer than scattered paper and spreadsheets, because you know where everything is and who can reach it. The key is choosing a provider you can talk to, and running your own team with clear access rules.
