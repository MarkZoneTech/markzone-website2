In salons, barbershops and many retail shops, commission is a big part of what staff earn. It motivates them to serve well and to sell. But when commissions are calculated by hand, from notebooks and memory, they become a monthly source of stress. One mistake, or even the suspicion of one, can damage trust with a good employee.

The fix is not complicated: clear rules, accurate records and consistent calculation.

## Why disputes happen

Most commission arguments come from the same few causes:

- **Missing records**: a service or sale was not written down, or was written under the wrong name.
- **Unclear rules**: staff are not sure whether products, packages or discounts count.
- **Manual calculation**: adding up a month of sales by hand invites errors.
- **Late surprises**: staff only see the number on payday, when it is too late to question details.

## Step 1: Write down the rules

Before any system, agree the rules and share them with the team:

- What percentage applies to services, and to products?
- How are discounts treated?
- How are packages and gift cards counted?
- What happens with shared services or when a client changes stylist?

Simple, written rules prevent most arguments before they start.

## Step 2: Record who did what, every time

Every service and product sale should be recorded against the staff member who performed or sold it, at the time it happens. Not at the end of the day, and not from memory. When the record is created at checkout, it is accurate.

## Step 4: Review regularly, not just on payday

Look at commission figures during the month, not only at the end. It lets you spot mistakes early and gives staff a chance to raise questions calmly. Regular reviews also show who is performing well and who might need support.
