The moment a customer reaches the counter is the moment they have already decided to buy. They trust you enough to pay. That makes checkout the easiest place to add a little more value to each sale, without spending anything on advertising.

Done badly, upselling feels pushy. Done well, it feels like good service. Here is how to get it right.

## Upsell vs. cross-sell

- **Upselling** means offering a better or larger version of what the customer is buying, such as the bigger pack or the premium option.
- **Cross-selling** means suggesting something that goes with it, such as batteries with a toy, a care product with a garment, or a treatment with a haircut.

For most small shops and salons, cross-selling is the easier win. It is natural, useful and quick.

## Habit 1: Pick a few "partner" items

Do not ask your staff to remember hundreds of combinations. Choose a short list of obvious pairs for your best-selling items, and place those partner items close to the counter. A clear, short list is easier to train and easier to follow.

Your sales data should guide the list. Look at what customers already tend to buy together, and which low-cost items sell well at the counter.

## Habit 2: Ask one relevant question

A single natural sentence works better than a script: "Do you have the matching one?" or "Would you like the refill as well?" Ask once, accept a no politely, and move on. The goal is to be helpful, not to win every time.

## Habit 3: Keep add-ons in stock

Nothing kills upselling faster than suggesting something you do not have. Put low-stock alerts on your add-on items so they are always available. In [OxPOS](/oxpos), low-stock alerts work on any item you choose.

## Habit 4: Use bundles and simple discounts carefully

A small discount on a bundle can move slower items along with popular ones. Keep it simple: one bundle, one clear price. Too many offers confuse customers and slow the queue. OxPOS supports item and invoice discounts, so staff apply them consistently instead of improvising.

## Habit 5: Make it part of the team’s routine

Staff upsell more when they know it is noticed. Share a simple weekly number with the team, such as how many add-on items were sold. Sales by employee show who is doing it well, so they can share what works with the others. Keep it positive and fair.

## Measure the results

Every month, check:

- **Average sale value**, and whether it is moving
- **Sales of your partner items**
- **Sales by employee**, to see who needs coaching

If a pairing does not sell, change it. Upselling is a small experiment you can run every week at no cost.
