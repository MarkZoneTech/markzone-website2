For most shops, inventory is the biggest amount of money sitting still. Every item on a shelf is cash you have already spent, waiting to come back. When stock expires, gets damaged, or sits unsold for months, that cash does not come back.

The good news is that most inventory waste comes from a few predictable habits, and fixing them does not require a warehouse team. Here are three that make the biggest difference for UAE retailers.

## 1. Stop buying from memory

The most common cause of waste is simple: ordering based on what feels right. The supplier visits, you look at the shelf, and you reorder roughly what you bought last time. Slow items get topped up anyway. Fast items get the same quantity even though they ran out last week.

Replace the feeling with your own numbers. Before placing an order, look at:

- **What actually sold** in the last few weeks, item by item
- **What is still on the shelf**, not what you think is there
- **What is close to expiry**, so you push it before buying more

A system that records every sale against stock gives you these answers in seconds. In [OxPOS](/oxpos), sales-by-item and stock-value reports sit next to your purchase records, so an order becomes a decision instead of a guess.

## 2. Set minimum levels and let alerts do the watching

You cannot check every shelf every day, and you should not have to. For each item that matters, decide the lowest quantity you are comfortable holding. When stock drops below that point, you want a warning, not a surprise.

Minimum levels work in both directions:

- **Fast movers** get reordered before they run out, so you stop losing sales to empty shelves.
- **Slow movers** get a low minimum, so you stop tying up cash in stock that barely moves.

OxPOS includes low-stock alerts built on exactly this idea. Review the levels every season. What sells in summer is not what sells in the cooler months or during Ramadan.

## 3. Treat expiry dates as a sales tool

For groceries, cosmetics, and anything with a shelf life, expiry is where waste becomes visible. The trick is to see it coming early enough to act.

Build a simple routine:

1. **Check what expires in the next 30 to 60 days** every week.
2. **Move those items forward**, onto the counter, into bundles, or into a small discount before they become a loss.
3. **Buy less of those items next time**, because they are telling you the order was too big.

OxPOS tracks expiry, so near-dated stock is something you find in a report rather than on a shelf after the date has passed.

## Bonus: variants and weighed products

Two areas trip up many shops. Fashion and garment stores carry the same item in many sizes and colours, and waste hides in the sizes that never sell. Grocery and food stores sell by weight, where small errors add up. A system that handles size and colour variants and weighed products properly shows you exactly which size or which product is the problem.

## Make it a habit, not a project

Reducing inventory waste is not a one-time clean-up. It is a weekly routine: check what sold, check what is low, check what is expiring, then order. With the right system, that routine takes minutes.
