A growing number of UAE stores now host home businesses and small vendors: homemade food, perfumes, abayas, accessories, gifts. It is a great model. Customers find something new every week, vendors get a shop window, and you earn from every item sold.

The difficulty starts the day after. Whose item is this? How many sold? What do I owe? What does she owe me? If those answers live in a notebook and in your memory, the store becomes stressful quickly.

## 1. Agree the terms clearly before the first item arrives

Decide how each vendor pays: a monthly rent, a commission on sales, or a combination. Write it down, share it, and keep a copy. Most disputes with vendors are not about money. They are about unclear expectations.

## 2. Give every vendor their own record

Each vendor needs a clear record: what she brought, what has sold, what is left, and what is owed. OxPOS has a consignment module built for stores that host home businesses and small vendors, so rent, commission and settlements are tracked per vendor instead of in a shared notebook.

## 3. Label every item so it belongs to someone

An item with no label is a problem waiting to happen. Give each product a barcode and a clear label so the cashier knows exactly whose item it is when it is scanned. OxPOS supports barcode scanning and has a custom label designer, so your labels can look consistent and professional.

## 4. Sell everything through one till

Customers should not have to pay different vendors separately. One point of sale for the whole store keeps checkout fast, and every sale is recorded. You can still handle returns, discounts and split payments in the usual way.

## 5. Settle on a fixed day

Vendors who are paid on a predictable day trust you more. Pick a settlement day, for example once a month, and stick to it. With each vendor's sales and balance already recorded, preparing the settlement takes minutes, not an afternoon of adding up receipts.

## 6. Keep your store's money separate from vendors' money

The cash in your drawer is not all yours. Part of it belongs to vendors. Keep your own numbers clear: your sales, your rent and commission income, your purchases and your expenses. Shift and cash control with expected versus actual cash at closing helps you see where you stand every day.

## 7. Give vendors a clear report

A vendor who can see exactly what sold is a vendor who stays. Reports can be exported as PDF or Excel, so you can share a clean statement at settlement time. It also shows you which vendors and products bring customers in, which helps you decide who to keep and what to promote.
