When the internet drops at the counter, everything slows down. Card payments hang, staff wait, the queue grows and customers leave. Many of these problems have nothing to do with the software. They come from a weak or unstable connection.

A few decisions about how your counter connects to the internet make a big difference.

## Wired vs. WiFi: the basic trade-off

**A wired connection** (a network cable from your router to the device) is generally more stable. It does not suffer from walls, distance, interference or a crowded signal. It is ideal for devices that never move: the main counter computer, a back-office PC, a fixed printer.

**WiFi** is convenient and flexible. It is the right choice for devices that move around the shop, like tablets or phones. But it can weaken with distance, thick walls, metal shelving, and interference from other networks nearby, which is common in malls and busy commercial buildings.

A simple rule: **wire what stays still, use WiFi for what moves.**

## Signs your WiFi is not good enough at the counter

- Pages or screens load slowly at certain times of day
- Payments or sync sometimes hang, then work on the second try
- The problem gets worse when the shop is busy
- The counter is far from the router or behind a wall

If you recognise these, try a wired connection for the counter device before blaming anything else.

## Place the router properly

Many shop routers sit wherever the installer left them: in a storeroom, behind metal shelving, or on the floor. Move it somewhere central and open, off the floor, and away from large metal objects if you can. If your shop is large, ask your internet provider about extending coverage properly rather than relying on a weak signal.

## Separate guest WiFi from business WiFi

If you offer WiFi to customers, give them a separate guest network. Keep your business devices on their own network with a strong password. It is better for security, and it stops customer phones competing with your counter for bandwidth.

## Have a backup plan

Every connection fails sometimes. Plan for it:

- **Keep your provider’s support number** visible at the counter.
- **Consider a mobile data backup**, such as a phone hotspot, for essential devices.
- **Restart the router properly** before assuming a bigger problem.
- **Know who to call** for what: your internet provider for the connection, your software provider for the system.
