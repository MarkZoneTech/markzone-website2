Many UAE shops now sell in two places: in the shop and online. The challenge appears quickly. An item sells at the counter, but the website still shows it as available. An online order arrives for something that is no longer on the shelf. The customer is disappointed, and your team spends time apologising instead of selling.

Connecting your online sales and your shop can solve this, but it is worth understanding what is involved before you start.

## What "connecting" really means

When people talk about connecting a website to a shop system, they usually mean one or more of these:

- **Stock**: when an item sells in one place, availability updates in the other.
- **Products and prices**: one list of products and prices used everywhere.
- **Orders**: online orders appear where the shop team can prepare them.
- **Customers**: one record of each customer, whether they buy online or in store.

Each of these is a separate piece of work. You may not need all of them.

## Start with the problem, not the technology

Before discussing any technical solution, write down the actual problems:

- How often do online customers order something that is out of stock?
- How much time does your team spend updating the website by hand?
- Do prices online and in store ever differ?

If these happen rarely, a simple routine may be enough. If they happen every week, a proper connection may be worth it.

## Get the foundations right first

Any connection is only as good as the data behind it. If your shop’s stock is not accurate, connecting it to a website simply shows the wrong numbers faster. Before any integration:

1. **Record every in-store sale against stock.** [OxPOS](/oxpos) tracks inventory with variants and low-stock alerts.
2. **Keep one clean product list**, with consistent names, sizes and prices.
3. **Agree who is responsible** for online orders and stock updates.
