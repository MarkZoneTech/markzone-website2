Ramadan is one of the busiest and most predictable seasons in UAE retail. Shopping patterns change. Evening trade grows, certain categories surge, and the final days before Eid bring their own rush. Shops that plan well sell more and waste less. Shops that plan from memory end up with empty shelves in week one and leftover stock after Eid.

The difference is almost always data. Here is a simple way to prepare.

## Start with last year’s numbers

Your best forecast is your own history. Before you speak to a single supplier, look at what happened last Ramadan:

- **Which items sold out**, and how early
- **Which items did not move**, despite being ordered in bulk
- **How sales changed week by week**, from the first days to the last days before Eid
- **Which hours were busiest**, so you know when you need more staff

If every sale was recorded by item, this takes minutes. In [OxPOS](/oxpos), sales reports by item, employee and receipt show exactly what moved and when. If you are still using a register with no item detail, this is the season that shows why it matters.

## Build your order in three groups

Split your products into three simple groups:

1. **Ramadan heroes**: the items that clearly jumped last year. Order these early, because suppliers get busy too.
2. **Steady sellers**: normal demand, normal orders. Do not over-order them just because it is a busy month.
3. **Slow or seasonal risks**: items that sat on the shelf last time. Order carefully, or not at all.

This avoids the most common mistake: increasing everything by the same amount "because Ramadan is busy."

## Watch expiry dates closely

Many Ramadan categories are perishable or have limited shelf life. Buying extra only helps if you can sell it in time. Check expiry dates on current stock before adding more, and plan to move near-dated items early in the month rather than after Eid. OxPOS tracks expiry alongside stock, so this check becomes part of your weekly routine.

## Plan staff and shifts, not just stock

More customers in the evening means your busiest hours shift. Look at last year’s sales by hour and by employee to decide:

- Who needs to be on the till during peak hours
- Whether you need an extra person for the last ten days
- How to handle opening and closing cash on longer shifts

Shift and cash control, with opening and closing cash and expected versus actual amounts, keeps busy nights from turning into long reconciliations.

## Keep cash flow in view

Buying heavily before Ramadan ties up cash before the sales arrive. Keep an eye on your supplier invoices, expenses and salaries alongside expected sales, so a strong month does not create a tight one. When purchases and expenses live in the same system as sales, you can see the full picture instead of guessing.

## After Eid: learn for next year

The work does not end on Eid. Take thirty minutes to note what sold out, what was left over, and what you would change. That review is what makes next Ramadan easier.
