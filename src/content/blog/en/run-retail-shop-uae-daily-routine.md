Ask most shop owners how the shop is doing and they will say "okay, alhamdulillah." Ask what the real profit was last month and the answer gets harder. Sales are in one place, purchases in another, expenses in a notebook, salaries somewhere else. A shop can be busy all day and still not know whether it is making money.

The fix is not a bigger plan. It is a small routine, repeated every day. Here is one that works for UAE retail shops of any size.

## Start the day: open the shift and count the cash

Before the first sale, count the cash in the drawer and record it as the opening amount. This takes two minutes and gives you a starting point for the day. OxPOS has shift and cash control built in: opening cash, closing cash, and a comparison of expected versus actual cash at the end of the shift.

## During the day: record every sale, return and discount

The rule is simple: if money or stock moves, it goes through the system. That includes returns and discounts. With barcode scanning, split payments, parked sales, returns and item or invoice discounts all in one point of sale, your staff can serve customers quickly and your records stay complete.

## Check stock every morning, not once a month

Empty shelves lose sales. Overstock ties up your cash. A quick look at the low-stock list each morning tells you what to reorder before you run out. If you sell food, cosmetics or anything with a date, expiry tracking shows what to move first. Size and colour variants and weighed products are handled in the same inventory, so the numbers match what is on the shelf.

## Record purchases and expenses the same day

When a supplier delivers, enter the purchase invoice right away. It keeps supplier records clean and shows what you actually paid. Do the same for daily expenses and salaries. Shops that write these down "later" usually discover at month-end that the profit was smaller than they thought. In OxPOS, purchases, suppliers, expenses and salaries sit in the same system as your sales.

## Close the day with the end-of-day report

At closing, count the cash and compare it with what the system expects. A small difference is normal. A repeated difference tells you something needs attention, such as a till error, a wrong change, or a process gap. Closing every day means a problem is found in a day, not in a month.

## Once a week: read three reports

You do not need to read everything. Start with three:

- **Sales by item:** what sells fast, and what is just sitting there.
- **Stock value:** how much of your money is on the shelves.
- **Profit and loss:** the real picture after purchases and expenses.

OxPOS also has reports for sales by employee and by receipt, payment types and VAT, with PDF and Excel export you can send to your accountant. For VAT rules and filing, follow the official guidance of the Federal Tax Authority.

## Once a month: review suppliers and customer balances

If you give credit to regular customers, customer accounts show balances and purchase history in one place, so you know who owes what. Use your supplier records to see who you buy most from, and whether it is time to talk about price.
