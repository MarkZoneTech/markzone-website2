Walk into almost any shop in the UAE and you will see customers paying in different ways: cash, card, phone. Many pay part in cash and part by card. The UAE has been steadily moving towards digital payments, and customers expect to pay however they prefer.

For shop owners, more payment methods bring a practical challenge: keeping the records straight. When cash, card and other payments mix at the same till, end-of-day totals become harder to check. Here is how to stay organized.

## Why mixed payments cause confusion

Problems usually appear at closing time:

- The cash in the drawer does not match what was expected.
- A card payment was recorded as cash, or the other way round.
- A customer paid in two parts, but only one part was recorded.
- Nobody is sure which staff member handled a disputed sale.

Each small error takes time to investigate and can create suspicion within the team.

## 1. Record every payment type at the moment of sale

The single most important habit is to record exactly how each sale was paid, at the till, when it happens. If a customer pays partly in cash and partly by card, record both parts. [OxPOS](/oxpos) supports split payments, so a mixed payment is recorded correctly instead of being forced into one method.

## 2. Use opening and closing cash

Start every shift by counting and recording the opening cash, and end it the same way. When the system knows the opening amount and the cash sales, it knows how much cash should be in the drawer. OxPOS shift and cash control compares expected versus actual amounts and produces an end-of-day report.

## 3. Check payment-type reports daily

A daily look at sales by payment type shows whether totals make sense. If card sales look unusually low one day, or cash unusually high, you can check quickly while everyone still remembers the day. OxPOS reports include payment types alongside sales by item, employee and receipt.

## 4. Link sales to staff

When each sale is linked to the staff member who made it, discrepancies can be traced calmly and fairly. Sales by employee turn a vague problem into a specific question.

## 5. Know your fees

Digital payments often come with fees. Knowing what they cost you helps you understand your real margin. In [TexPOS](/texpos), reports include payment-gateway fees, shown to you only and never to the customer.

## 6. Keep VAT clean

Whatever the payment method, VAT must be correct. VAT-ready reports that already split your sales make handing numbers to your accountant straightforward.
